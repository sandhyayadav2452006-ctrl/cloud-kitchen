## Project Overview

**TasteNest** is a conceptual cloud-kitchen brand created to demonstrate how food can be prepared in a kitchen and sold to customers through an online ordering experience. Unlike a traditional restaurant, a cloud kitchen focuses on preparing food for delivery or pickup and does not require a dine-in seating area.

This project presents the idea from both a **business perspective** and a **software-system perspective**. It explains the customer ordering journey, kitchen-side order management, possible income sources, cost considerations, and a proposed design using Java classes and database entities.

> **Academic prototype notice:** TasteNest is a hypothetical brand created for demonstration. It is not a real or registered business. Screens, menu prices, order IDs, and sample dashboard counts shown in the presentation are illustrative. The presentation describes Java classes and database entities conceptually; it does not by itself establish that a production backend, live delivery service, or payment gateway has been implemented.

## Problem Statement

A person may be able to prepare good food but may not have the money or space to open a full dine-in restaurant. A cloud kitchen offers another business approach: concentrate on food preparation, packaging, online visibility, order management, and delivery instead of investing in a dining hall.

The project explores these questions:

- Can food be sold without a dine-in restaurant?
- Can customers place orders using a phone or online platform?
- How can a small kitchen be organized as an online business?
- How can an order be managed from placement to delivery?

## What Is a Cloud Kitchen?

A **cloud kitchen** is a food business that prepares meals for delivery or pickup without providing a dine-in seating area. Its work is organized around food preparation and receiving orders through digital channels.

![Cloud kitchen concept and comparison](assets/cloud-kitchen-concept.png)

### Traditional Restaurant vs. Cloud Kitchen

| Aspect | Traditional restaurant | Cloud kitchen |
|---|---|---|
| Setup focus | Dining space, interiors, furniture, and kitchen | Kitchen facilities, packaging, and order fulfilment |
| Customer interaction | Walk-in and dine-in service as well as other channels | Primarily online orders and delivery/pickup |
| Location | Often seeks a high-footfall commercial location | Can use a legally suitable kitchen location |
| Team focus | May include hosts, waiters, servers, and chefs | Focuses on cooking, packing, and dispatch |
| Growth approach | May expand through additional physical branches | May expand through online listings and delivery coverage |

A cloud kitchen still needs suitable food-preparation facilities, hygiene standards, applicable food-safety compliance, and organized daily operations. Removing the dining area does not remove the responsibilities of running a food business.

## How Online Ordering Works

The typical workflow shown in the project is:

![Online food ordering workflow](assets/ordering-workflow.png)

1. The customer opens the platform.
2. The customer views restaurants and menu items.
3. The customer selects food items.
4. Selected items are added to the cart.
5. The customer confirms the address and payment method.
6. The order reaches the kitchen.
7. The kitchen accepts or rejects the order.
8. The food is prepared and packed.
9. Delivery is coordinated.
10. The order is delivered, after which the customer may provide feedback or a rating.

## Business and Income Model

The project explains the relationship between the kitchen, digital platform, customers, and delivery. Possible revenue sources include:

### Revenue vs. Profit

**Revenue** is the total money received from selling food. **Profit** is what remains after total business expenses are subtracted.

**Profit = Revenue − Total Expenses**

Expenses may include ingredients, packaging, delivery, kitchen operations, and other applicable business costs. High sales do not automatically mean high profit. Income is not guaranteed and depends on demand, pricing, expenses, and service quality.

## Proposed Java Classes and Database Design

![Proposed Java classes and database entities](assets/java-database-design.png)

The presentation proposes the following classes/entities for an order-management system:

| Class / entity | Example fields | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `Customer` | `id`, `name`, `address`, `phone` | Represents a customer and their contact/delivery details |
| `MenuItem` | `itemId`, `name`, `price`, `available` | Represents a menu item and its availability |
| `Order` | `orderId`, `customerId`, `status`, `total` | Represents an order placed by a customer |
| `OrderItem` | `orderId`, `itemId`, `quantity` | Connects an order with its selected items and quantities |
| `Admin` | `adminId`, `name`, `role` | Represents an administrator or authorized staff member |
| `Payment` | Conceptual class | Represents payment-related information in the proposed design |

Possible relational database tables include `Customer`, `MenuItem`, `Orders`, `OrderItems`, and `Admin`. Identifiers can link an order to a customer and connect it to multiple order items.

These are the design concepts presented in the project. The actual implementation details should be documented separately if a working Java application or database is included in the repository.

## Key Learnings

- A cloud kitchen is a delivery-focused food business model.
- Digital ordering platforms can connect kitchens with customers.
- Customer and kitchen/admin interfaces support different parts of the order lifecycle.
- Revenue and profit are different; expenses must be tracked.
- Food quality, hygiene, customer satisfaction, and cost management matter.
- Java classes and database entities can be planned to organize customers, menu items, and orders.
- A real business requires planning, validation, and responsible operations.

## Conclusion

TasteNest demonstrates the idea of turning a kitchen into an online food-ordering business without a dine-in restaurant. The project brings together the business model, customer ordering journey, kitchen operations, admin-side order management, marketing, and a proposed Java/database design.

It highlights that a cloud kitchen is not only about selling food online: it also requires quality food preparation, organized order handling, cost control, and customer trust.
